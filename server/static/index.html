Climate Science Impact

Polar Cryosphere Teleconnections & Indian Climate

Changes in high-latitude polar ice sheets and Arctic sea-ice extent directly modulate monsoon intensity, sea-level rise along India's coastline, and ocean carbon sink capacity.

Sea-Level Rise on India's 7,516 km Coastline

Runoff and mass discharge from Antarctic ice shelves directly determine sea-surface heights across Mumbai, Chennai, and Kolkata. Precision monitoring enables climate-resilient coastal defense planning.

Vulnerability: Low-Lying Deltas & Coastal Megacities

Arctic Sea-Ice Loss & Monsoon Modulation

Springtime sea-ice reduction in the Barents-Kara Seas excites planetary Rossby wave trains propagating across Eurasia, altering upper-tropospheric thermal gradients that influence Indian Summer Monsoon rainfall.

Impact: Precipitation Dynamics & Agricultural Planning

Southern Ocean Planetary Carbon Sink

The Southern Ocean absorbs nearly 40% of all oceanic anthropogenic carbon dioxide. Indian expedition cruises on ORV Sagar Nidhi track air-sea pCO₂ fluxes to quantify global ocean climate regulation.

Global Balance: 40% Anthropogenic Oceanic CO₂

Institutional Mandate

About the National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research

Autonomous research institution under the Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES), Government of India, coordinating all Indian scientific missions across polar and oceanic realms.

Our Mission & Leadership

Headquartered in Headland Sada, Vasco-da-Gama, Goa, NCPOR serves as the nodal agency for planning, executing, and steering India's research in Antarctica, the Arctic, the Southern Ocean, and the Himalayas.

  • Conducting continuous multi-disciplinary earth and atmospheric science observation.
  • Operating year-round state-of-the-art research bases (Bharati, Maitri, Himadri, Himansh).
  • Archiving ice-core records in national sub-zero paleoclimate vaults.
  • Leading India's consultative role in the Antarctic Treaty System and Arctic Council.

Key Milestones Timeline

1981: 1st Indian Scientific Expedition to Antarctica led by Dr. S.Z. Qasim.
1983: Commissioning of Dakshin Gangotri & India joins Antarctic Treaty.
1989: Inauguration of permanent research station Maitri in Schirmacher Oasis.
2008: Inauguration of Arctic research station Himadri in Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard.
2012: Commissioning of state-of-the-art station Bharati on hydraulic stilts.
2014: Deployment of IndARC underwater moored observatory in Kongsfjorden.
2016: Inauguration of Himansh High-Altitude Station (4,050m) in the Himalayas.
2022: Enactment of the Indian Antarctic Act 2022.
2024: India hosts the 46th Antarctic Treaty Consultative Meeting (ATCM 46) in Kochi.
